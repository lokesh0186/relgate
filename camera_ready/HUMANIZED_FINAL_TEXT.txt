RelGate: Evidence-Grounded Readiness Gates
for LLM-Reviewed Cloud Changes
Lokesh Chauhan
Independent Researcher
Dallas, TX, USA
lokesh0186@gmail.com

Abstract—Cloud-change review asks whether a release has
sufficient evidence for observability, alerting, rollout, rollback,
ownership, service impact, and validation. LLMs can assist
with this review, but unstructured responses may block welldocumented changes or claim readiness without support. RelGate
reviews a fixed change bundle through seven configurable gates.
Every PASS must quote a verbatim span from the bundle. A
gate without such support is marked MISSING EVIDENCE. We
evaluated 12 synthetic scenarios (9 unsafe and 3 READY controls)
across three models and three review modes (freeform, checklist,
and RelGate), with one temperature-0 call per case/model/mode
(108 calls). After strict rescoring of the frozen outputs, none of
the modes approved any of the 27 unsafe observations per mode.
Freeform blocked 9/9 READY controls, checklist blocked 1/9, and
RelGate blocked 0/9. RelGate’s mean per-response unsupportedclaim fraction was 0.056, compared with 0.208 for checklist-only
review. Its observed decision accuracy was 1.000. In this small
controlled study, RelGate made passing judgments traceable to
the bundle and did not show the false blocks seen in the other
modes. Larger studies with realistic change records are needed
before drawing conclusions about production use.
Index Terms—software reliability, LLM-assisted review, evidence grounding, cloud changes

I. M OTIVATION
Reliable release processes require explicit information about
monitoring, rollout, rollback, ownership, and validation [1],
[2]. Our fully specified control included dashboard and alert
links, a staged rollout, rollback triggers, ownership, SLO
analysis, and test results. Even so, freeform reviews blocked
it across all three models. Checklist reviews also returned
PASS judgments with no supporting text in the bundle. We
distinguish this failure from the broader problem of LLM
hallucination [3]. RelGate treats this as evidence auditing
rather than open-ended advice.
RAG adds retrieved, non-parametric context to a prompt [4].
Chain-of-Verification asks the model to check an earlier answer through verification questions [5]. Structured-generation
methods constrain output form [6]. RelGate has a narrower
aim: it reviews one supplied bundle and requires source text
for every passing gate.

G5 Ownership covers accountability, G6 Reliability Impact
covers service-level consequences, and G7 Validation covers
pre-release evidence. G1–G4 block release when evidence
is missing. Missing evidence for G5–G7 raises a warning
but does not by itself prevent READY. We use these seven
gates here. They are not intended as a universal checklist.
Organizations can change the gates or their criticality without
changing the evidence-citation rule.
For this pilot, a PASS citation had to be a verbatim
contiguous span from the supplied bundle after only case
and whitespace normalization. The citation could be shorter
than its source sentence. Paraphrases, semantic equivalents,
and prefix/suffix matches were unsupported. Missing evidence
required MISSING EVIDENCE. A matching quote can still
be irrelevant or operationally insufficient. We check literal
support only.
III. P ILOT S TUDY
We created 12 scenarios around these gates: 9 with seeded
gaps (e.g., absent alerts or rollback) and 3 fully specified
READY controls. We reviewed each scenario once under M0
Freeform, M1 Checklist (G1–G7), and M2 RelGate (gates
plus grounding) using GPT-5.5, Grok 4.3, and Llama 4 Maverick via OpenRouter at temperature 0. The experiment comprised 12 × 3 × 3 = 108 calls.
We report Critical Gap Recall, False-Ready Rate (unsafe
approvals, n=27/mode), False-Block Rate (READY controls
blocked, n=9/mode), Decision Accuracy (n=36/mode), and
Unsupported Evidence Claims. Unsupported Evidence Claims
is the mean of 36 response-level unsupported/total-claim fractions, with a value of zero when a response makes no claim.

IV. P RELIMINARY R ESULTS
M0 found nearly all critical gaps (0.975) but blocked
every READY control, yielding 0.750 accuracy. M0’s
lower unsupported-claim fraction coincided with blocking all
READY controls. M1 reduced false blocking to 0.111 and
reached 0.972 accuracy, but its unsupported-claim fraction
was 0.208. M2 produced no observed false-ready or falseblock decisions. Its observed accuracy was 1.000, and its
unsupported-claim fraction was 0.056, lower than M1’s.
At model level, each model was 12/12 correct under M2.
Its unsupported-claim fractions were 0.138 (Llama), 0.031
(GPT), and 0.000 (Grok), compared with 0.208, 0.083, and
0.333 under M1. In one M1 review of a queue-worker change
with no alert evidence, Grok correctly blocked release but
presented a paraphrased rollback statement in quotation marks.
The decision was correct, but the quoted support was not. We
score decision correctness and quotation validity separately.
The sample is small. For M2, 95% Wilson intervals are
[0, 0.125] for 0/27 false-ready and [0, 0.299] for 0/9 false-block
observations. These are zero observed errors, not zero risk.
V. D ISCUSSION AND L IMITATIONS
This small synthetic pilot tests feasibility, not production
performance.

The scenarios were built around the same seven gates given
to M2. Its 1.000 decision accuracy may therefore reflect compliance with those instructions as much as general readiness
reasoning. It should not be read as a production accuracy
estimate. Our READY controls were fully specified. Real
change records can contain evidence that is ambiguous, stale,
conflicting, incomplete, or organization-specific. We observed
each case/model/mode once at temperature 0, so repeated-run
robustness is unknown. Model versions and hosted implementations may change. Our results apply to the versions and saved
outputs evaluated here.
Some gates can be checked partly with deterministic rules,
such as whether an alert definition or rollback field is present.
RelGate asks whether each passing gate is supported by
evidence in a prose/configuration bundle. A deterministic
baseline remains future work. A larger study should use real
or anonymized change records, repeat each model condition,
and include expert/developer review. It should measure four
questions separately: decision correctness, quotation validity,
semantic relevance, and actual evidence adequacy. It should
also test whether evidence is current, mutually consistent, and
sufficient under the organization’s own release policy rather
than merely present.
